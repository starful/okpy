## Effect snapshot

| | |
|--|--|
| Intervention | Stop phone use 60 minutes before bed vs using until lights out |
| Outcome | Time to fall asleep |
| Effect | 10–20 percent relative decrease |
| Confidence | `study` |
| Context | Adults 18–35, self-reported and wearable sleep, 2020–2025 |

### Sources

- [Sleep Foundation — Screen time](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/)

## What changes

A screen-free wind-down hour is often associated with a 10–20% relative reduction in time to fall asleep in short interventions.

## When this tends to work

- Conditions similar to: Adults 18–35, self-reported and wearable sleep, 2020–2025
- You can measure `Time to fall asleep` reliably
- The intervention is implemented consistently, not half-measured

## When to be careful

- Your audience or product differs materially from the cited context
- Compliance costs or second-order effects outweigh the lift
- Evidence label is **study**—treat wide ranges as planning bands, not promises

## Practical takeaway

Use the cited range for prioritization and test design. Verify against your own data before scaling.
