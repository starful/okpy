## Effect snapshot

| | |
|--|--|
| Intervention | Book domestic flights 6–8 weeks before departure vs 1 week before |
| Outcome | Average fare paid per route |
| Effect | 10–25 percent relative decrease |
| Confidence | `study` |
| Context | US domestic routes, economy class, 2019–2025 fare studies |

### Sources

- [Google Flights — Price insights](https://www.google.com/travel/flights)

## What changes

Booking several weeks ahead is commonly associated with 10–25% lower average fares than one-week-out purchases on domestic routes.

## When this tends to work

- Conditions similar to: US domestic routes, economy class, 2019–2025 fare studies
- You can measure `Average fare paid per route` reliably
- The intervention is implemented consistently, not half-measured

## When to be careful

- Your audience or product differs materially from the cited context
- Compliance costs or second-order effects outweigh the lift
- Evidence label is **study**—treat wide ranges as planning bands, not promises

## Practical takeaway

Use the cited range for prioritization and test design. Verify against your own data before scaling.
