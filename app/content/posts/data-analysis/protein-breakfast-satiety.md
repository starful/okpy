## Effect snapshot

| | |
|--|--|
| Intervention | Eat 25–30g protein at breakfast vs carb-heavy breakfast |
| Outcome | Calories from snacks before dinner |
| Effect | 12–22 percent relative decrease |
| Confidence | `study` |
| Context | Adults in weight-management studies, 2–4 week protocols |

### Sources

- [NIH — Protein and satiety](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/)

## What changes

Higher-protein breakfasts are commonly linked to a 12–22% relative reduction in afternoon snack calories in short trials.

## When this tends to work

- Conditions similar to: Adults in weight-management studies, 2–4 week protocols
- You can measure `Calories from snacks before dinner` reliably
- The intervention is implemented consistently, not half-measured

## When to be careful

- Your audience or product differs materially from the cited context
- Compliance costs or second-order effects outweigh the lift
- Evidence label is **study**—treat wide ranges as planning bands, not promises

## Practical takeaway

Use the cited range for prioritization and test design. Verify against your own data before scaling.
