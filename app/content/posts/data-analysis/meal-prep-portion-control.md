## Effect snapshot

| | |
|--|--|
| Intervention | Pre-portion lunches into containers on Sunday vs eating ad hoc servings |
| Outcome | Average daily calorie intake |
| Effect | 8–15 percent relative decrease |
| Confidence | `study` |
| Context | Office workers meal-prepping, 4-week interventions, 2020–2025 |

### Sources

- [NIH — Portion control](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/educational/wecan/eat-right/portion-distortion.htm)

## What changes

Pre-portioned meal prep is often associated with an 8–15% relative reduction in average daily calories in short interventions.

## When this tends to work

- Conditions similar to: Office workers meal-prepping, 4-week interventions, 2020–2025
- You can measure `Average daily calorie intake` reliably
- The intervention is implemented consistently, not half-measured

## When to be careful

- Your audience or product differs materially from the cited context
- Compliance costs or second-order effects outweigh the lift
- Evidence label is **study**—treat wide ranges as planning bands, not promises

## Practical takeaway

Use the cited range for prioritization and test design. Verify against your own data before scaling.
