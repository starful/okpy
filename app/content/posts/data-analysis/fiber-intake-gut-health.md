## Effect snapshot

| | |
|--|--|
| Intervention | Increase daily fiber from ~15g to 30g via whole foods |
| Outcome | Self-reported bowel regularity scores |
| Effect | 15–35 percent relative increase |
| Confidence | `study` |
| Context | Adults with mild irregularity, 4–8 week dietary interventions |

### Sources

- [Harvard TH Chan — Fiber](https://www.hsph.harvard.edu/nutritionsource/carbohydrates/fiber/)

## What changes

Doubling fiber toward 30g/day is commonly associated with a 15–35% relative improvement in regularity scores in short dietary studies.

## When this tends to work

- Conditions similar to: Adults with mild irregularity, 4–8 week dietary interventions
- You can measure `Self-reported bowel regularity scores` reliably
- The intervention is implemented consistently, not half-measured

## When to be careful

- Your audience or product differs materially from the cited context
- Compliance costs or second-order effects outweigh the lift
- Evidence label is **study**—treat wide ranges as planning bands, not promises

## Practical takeaway

Use the cited range for prioritization and test design. Verify against your own data before scaling.
