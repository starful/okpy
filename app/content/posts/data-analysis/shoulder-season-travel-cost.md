## Effect snapshot

| | |
|--|--|
| Intervention | Travel in shoulder season vs peak holiday week |
| Outcome | Total trip cost (lodging + flights) |
| Effect | 15–35 percent relative decrease |
| Confidence | `estimate` |
| Context | European city breaks and US national parks, 2022–2025 |

### Sources

- [Skyscanner — Best time to book](https://www.skyscanner.com/tips-and-inspiration/)

## What changes

Shoulder-season trips are often 15–35% cheaper than peak-week equivalents for the same destinations.

## When this tends to work

- Conditions similar to: European city breaks and US national parks, 2022–2025
- You can measure `Total trip cost (lodging + flights)` reliably
- The intervention is implemented consistently, not half-measured

## When to be careful

- Your audience or product differs materially from the cited context
- Compliance costs or second-order effects outweigh the lift
- Evidence label is **estimate**—treat wide ranges as planning bands, not promises

## Practical takeaway

Use the cited range for prioritization and test design. Verify against your own data before scaling.
