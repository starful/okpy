## Effect snapshot

| | |
|--|--|
| Intervention | Add a persistent sticky CTA bar on mobile landing pages |
| Outcome | Primary CTA click-through rate |
| Effect | 10–22 percent relative increase |
| Confidence | `ab_test` |
| Context | Mobile landing pages, lead gen and app install, 2023–2025 |

### Sources

- [CXL — Mobile CTA patterns](https://cxl.com/blog/mobile-cta/)

## What changes

Sticky mobile CTAs are often reported to lift primary CTA clicks by roughly 10–22% relative on long-scroll pages.

## When this tends to work

- Conditions similar to: Mobile landing pages, lead gen and app install, 2023–2025
- You can measure `Primary CTA click-through rate` reliably
- The intervention is implemented consistently, not half-measured

## When to be careful

- Your audience or product differs materially from the cited context
- Compliance costs or second-order effects outweigh the lift
- Evidence label is **ab_test**—treat wide ranges as planning bands, not promises

## Practical takeaway

Use the cited range for prioritization and test design. Verify against your own data before scaling.
