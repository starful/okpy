## What changes
This insight examines the impact of integrating a straightforward "Party Up" button directly onto the post-match scoreboard. Specifically targeting solo players, this feature allows them to instantly group with teammates they've just played with, removing common friction points in forming a new party. Rather than navigating menus, sending friend requests, or manually inviting, the intervention provides a seamless, one-click solution to continue playing together. The aim is to capitalize on positive social interactions immediately after a game concludes, encouraging continued engagement through shared experiences.

## When this tends to work
This approach is particularly effective in multiplayer online games where social interaction and teamwork are integral to the gameplay experience. For solo players who might otherwise log off after a match, a positive recent game can be a strong catalyst for continued play if the path to do so is frictionless. By facilitating immediate group formation, this feature taps into the desire for social connection and reduces the psychological barrier of finding new teammates for the next match. Data from A/B tests indicates that implementing such a button can increase Day-7 player retention by an impressive 8 to 15 percent relative. This uplift is primarily observed in environments where players are frequently matched with strangers and where the game benefits from coordinated play, fostering a sense of community and shared accomplishment.

## When to be careful
While generally beneficial, this intervention may have limited impact or even unintended consequences in certain scenarios. Games with highly competitive ranked ladders, for instance, might see players prioritize specific, pre-formed teams or individual skill assessment over impromptu group-ups. Similarly, in games where social interaction isn't a primary driver of engagement, or where players predominantly prefer a solitary experience even within a multiplayer context (e.g., some battle royales or free-for-alls), the effect on retention might be less pronounced. Developers should also consider the quality of potential group-ups; if the 'Party Up' system frequently pairs players who had a negative in-game experience, it could inadvertently discourage future play. The focus should always be on enhancing positive social interactions, not forcing them.

## Practical takeaway
For game developers and product managers focused on increasing player engagement and long-term retention in multiplayer titles, adding a one-click 'Party Up' button on the post-match scoreboard for solo players presents a high-impact, relatively low-effort solution. This simple user experience enhancement directly addresses a common friction point in social gaming, converting transient positive experiences into sustained social play. The evidence strongly supports its efficacy, showing an 8 to 15 percent relative increase in Day-7 player retention. By making it effortless for players to continue playing with compatible teammates, you not only improve individual player experience but also strengthen the social fabric of your game community, making players more likely to return.
