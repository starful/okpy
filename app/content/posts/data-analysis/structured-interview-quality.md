## Effect snapshot

| | |
|--|--|
| Intervention | Replace ad hoc interviews with scored structured question sets |
| Outcome | New-hire 12-month performance rating |
| Effect | 10–24 percent relative increase |
| Confidence | `meta_analysis` |
| Context | Mid-size companies, knowledge-worker roles, 2018–2025 |

### Sources

- [Google re:Work — Structured interviewing](https://rework.withgoogle.com/guides/hiring-use-structured-interviewing/)

## What changes

Structured interviews are often associated with a 10–24% relative improvement in subsequent job performance versus unstructured formats.

## When this tends to work

- Conditions similar to: Mid-size companies, knowledge-worker roles, 2018–2025
- You can measure `New-hire 12-month performance rating` reliably
- The intervention is implemented consistently, not half-measured

## When to be careful

- Your audience or product differs materially from the cited context
- Compliance costs or second-order effects outweigh the lift
- Evidence label is **meta_analysis**—treat wide ranges as planning bands, not promises

## Practical takeaway

Use the cited range for prioritization and test design. Verify against your own data before scaling.
