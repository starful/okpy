# Data Engineer vs Analyst vs Scientist (in one restaurant)

I keep meeting juniors who say they want to “go into data,” then get stuck choosing a title.

So I drew it as a restaurant:

![Three data roles as a restaurant](https://storage.googleapis.com/ok-project-assets/okpy/career/three-data-roles.jpg)

## The three roles

- **Data Engineer → kitchen.** Make the ingredients arrive clean and on time. Pipelines, quality, storage. If this breaks, nobody else can cook.
- **Data Analyst → the board.** Say what sold and what didn’t, so someone can decide. Metrics, reports, “so what?” If definitions are fuzzy, the board lies.
- **Data Scientist → the experiment table.** Test “what if we change the recipe?” Models, experiments, hypotheses. If the question is vague, the experiment is theater.

Same restaurant. Different jobs.

## How to choose early in your career

Pick the **pain you like solving**, not the fanciest title:

| If you enjoy… | Start closer to… |
|---|---|
| Making systems reliable so data shows up | Data Engineer |
| Turning numbers into a clear decision | Data Analyst |
| Designing tests and uncertain bets | Data Scientist |

Titles blur in real companies (and “analytics engineer” sits between kitchen and board). That is fine. The useful skill is knowing **which job you are doing today**.

## One tip before you chase tools

Learn the shared vocabulary of the business first: what people mean by conversion, retention, revenue. Tools change. Confused definitions do not wait for your stack.
